# Sales research (gathered 2026-10-14)

Everything here comes from web searches, mostly vendor blogs that sell answering services or AI receptionists. Treat numbers as directional, not proof. Verify competitor prices on their own sites before quoting them to a customer.

## 1. What a missed call is worth
- Estimates per missed call in home services: $200 to $500 immediate revenue (Signpost); $300 to $1,200 (JustCall citing Invoca); bigger jobs (HVAC replacement, repipe) $3,000 to $15,000.
- Missed-call rates reported: 14% (CallRail, Jan 2025), about 27% (Invoca 2024), 48% of calls not answered by a person (Invoca, July 2026), 74% in one vendor study of 45 contractors (plumbers best at about 28.6% missed). The sources disagree, so ask each prospect for their OWN numbers.
- Caveat from Signpost itself: a missed call is not automatically a lost job (spam, vendors, callers who accept a callback).
- Pitch math: missed calls per month x share that would have booked x average job value. Get the prospect's numbers on the first call.
- Sources: signpost.com/blog/how-much-business-do-contractors-lose-from-missed-calls, stealthagents.com/research/home-services-missed-call-revenue-statistics-2026, justcall.io/blog/call-center-for-contractors.html

## 2. What competitors charge (end customers)
- Generic AI receptionist SaaS: $25 to $100 (basic), $100 to $300 or $600 (standard, with scheduling), $1,000 to $3,000+ (high volume). A tradesperson-focused blog says roughly $29 to $300 a month (Jobber add-on about $29 for 30 conversations; standalone services $49 to $299).
- Example: Aibrify Desk $179/month, 400 minutes included.
- Watch for per-minute overage hidden in "flat" plans.
- Not found: usable current pricing for Smith.ai, Rosie, Housecall Pro AI. Check their pricing pages.
- Setup fees in the market: $0 to $4,999+; custom builds quoted at 2,000 to 4,000+.
- Sources: superdupr.com/blog/how-much-does-an-ai-receptionist-cost, aibrify.com/blog/ai-receptionist-for-hvac-and-plumbers, sagnikbhattacharya.com/blog/ai-receptionist-tradespeople-cost

## 3. Pricing implications for Carter (reasoning, not sourced)
- Carter's cost is about $0.13 to $0.31 per minute (Retell) plus about $2 per number plus a few dollars of n8n share. A 500-minute plan costs about $100 at $0.20.
- Competitors sit at $29 to $300. A done-for-you, calendar-booking, trade-specific setup can price above the cheap SaaS, but must justify it with the missed-call math.
- Avoid a plan whose included minutes cost more than half the price. Use tiers plus overage per minute rather than a hard stop.

## 4. How others sell this
- Target owner-operators and small shops without a front desk (a front desk sees you as competition).
- Lead with dollars per missed call, not technology.
- Let them hear it: a demo number, or call the prospect's own business after hours to show the problem.
- Free trial or pilot is commonly recommended.
- Objections to prepare: "we already have a receptionist", "AI is too expensive", "it will sound robotic".
- Vendor claims (30 to 50% missed calls, trial conversion rates) are unverified marketing numbers.
- Sources: growwstacks.com/blog/sell-ai-receptionists-to-local-businesses-with-cold-calls, growwstacks.com/blog/ai-receptionist-cold-call-script

## Competitor check: ai-receptionist.com realtor page (2026-10-23, from Carter's copy of their subscription page)

- NOT missed-calls-only. They say: "Forward your cell or office line, or only the calls you miss" and "Get a new number for a sign or an ad if you prefer". So they support both modes (answering every call on a dedicated number, or conditional forwarding for missed calls only).
- They sell by industry: separate pages and scripts for buyer calls, seller calls, rentals and property management (price questions, open house times, showing bookings, maintenance requests, urgent alerts on Starter and up, answers in the tenant's language).
- Features we do not have yet: texting the caller a link or details during the call, replying to texts, SMS alerts (all need Twilio); linked team accounts; transfer contacts (5 on Business, 20 on top plan).
- Their low prices come from self-serve setup and templates, not from limited features. Ours is done-for-you, per-business booking rules (several barbers at once, combos, cancel/reschedule), no hard stop at the limit.
- Lesson to copy: sell by industry with an industry-specific script and page (barbershops, repair shops, plumbers), not "AI receptionist for anyone".
