# Outreach pack: from first message to signed customer (2026-11-04)

Placeholders: [Name] owner, [Shop] business, [Your name] Carter. Never send a claim you can't back up. Use the owner's real numbers, not stats from vendors.

## 1. The path (one page)
1. **Reach out** with a short message that points to a working demo built on THEIR public info (menu, prices, hours).
2. **Short call or visit (10 min):** they call the demo from their own phone; you ask 6 qualifying questions.
3. **Yes / maybe / no.** Yes -> send the setup form + plan link. Maybe -> follow-up sequence. No -> thank them, ask who else might need it.
4. **Setup form (15-20 min)** -> you build -> you test with them -> they forward calls -> first week check-in.
5. **Month-end review** with their own call log. Ask for a testimonial.

## 2. First message options

### A. Email (best for shops with a public email)
Subject options: "I built a phone assistant for [Shop]" / "Missed calls at [Shop]?"

> Hi [Name],
>
> I'm [Your name]. I build phone assistants for local businesses. I noticed [Shop] books through [Square / your website], and when everyone is busy cutting hair, calls probably go unanswered.
>
> So I built a demo using your real services and prices. You can call it right now from your phone: **(219) 587-7319**. Ask for a haircut and beard trim on Saturday, ask how much a boys cut is, try to trip it up. It tells callers it's an automated assistant.
>
> It's a demo on a test calendar, so nothing touches your real bookings.
>
> If you like it, I can set it up on a free 10-minute call so it answers only the calls you miss. If not, no worries at all.
>
> [Your name]
> [phone] | [website]
> [Mailing address] | Not interested? Reply "stop" and I won't email again.

(The address and opt-out line are required for commercial email in the US, CAN-SPAM. Use a real address.)

### B. Text or Instagram DM (short)
> Hey [Name], it's [Your name]. I built a phone assistant that answers calls when your barbers are busy and books them. I made a demo using [Shop]'s real prices. Want to call it and see? (219) 587-7319. No pressure.

### C. Walk-in (best for a barbershop)
Go at a slow time (Tuesday-Thursday afternoon, not the Saturday rush). Ask for the owner or whoever handles the phone:
> "I'm [Your name], I build phone assistants for local shops. I made one for your shop using your Square prices. Can I show you in two minutes? Just call this number from your phone."
Leave a small card with the demo number and your email.

### D. Phone call
> "Hi, is this [Name]? I'm [Your name], not selling anything on this call. I built a free demo phone assistant for [Shop]. Could I text you the number so you can try it whenever you have a minute?"

## 3. Follow-up sequence (stop at the first reply)
- Day 3 (text or email): "Did you get a chance to call the demo? Happy to answer questions."
- Day 7: "One more idea: I can show you the call log so you can see how many calls [Shop] might be missing. 10 minutes?"
- Day 14: "Last note from me. If the timing's wrong, I get it. Mind if I check back in a couple of months?"
- Then stop. Never email someone who said stop.

## 4. Qualifying questions (first call, 10 minutes)
Ask, listen, write down their exact words.
1. When everyone's busy, what happens to the phone? Does it ring out, go to voicemail, or does someone stop to answer?
2. About how many calls do you think you miss in a week? When are the worst times?
3. How do customers usually book (phone, Square, walk-in, Instagram)?
4. What's a regular customer worth to you in a month?
5. Do you have anyone who'd answer calls if you could afford them?
6. What would make you comfortable trusting it with your customers? What would make you quit?
7. Who else would have to say yes?

**Buying signals:** counts missed calls without being asked, mentions losing customers, asks about price or "how soon," asks to try it with their own phone.
**Warning signs:** "we don't get many calls," "our customers all book online," no one answers the phone and they like it that way. Don't push; those shops are a poor fit.

## 5. The honest 30-second explanation
"When you can't pick up, a computer voice answers with your shop's name, tells callers it's an automated assistant, answers price and hours questions, books into your calendar, and emails you a summary. Anything it can't handle becomes a message. You keep your number. You can cancel anytime."

Do NOT promise: texts to customers (not live), writing into Square (not built), transfer to a person (untested), exact call counts or revenue gains.

## 6. Money conversation
Do the math WITH them, using their numbers:
(missed calls per week) x (share who'd book) x (average ticket) x 4 = monthly value.
Then the plans: Backup Lite 150 min $59, Backup Standard 300 min $89, Backup Plus 500 min $139 (setup $99; Spanish +$15; overage $0.40 per minute). Most one- to three-chair shops fit Backup Lite or Standard. Check `docs/pricing-guide.md` for the current list before quoting.

## 7. Objection answers
| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "I already answer my calls" | "Great. This only catches the ones nobody picks up: busy, closed, lunch." |
| "Will it sound like a robot?" | Let them call the demo. "It says it's automated if asked." |
| "What if it books wrong?" | "It only offers open times, and you get an email for every booking." |
| "I use Square" | "Today it books into a calendar. Writing straight into Square is a later step and I'd confirm it's possible before promising it." |
| "Too expensive" | Show the break-even: "$59 is about two haircuts a month." |
| "I need to think" | "Sure. Want me to leave the demo number up and check back next week?" |
| "My customers don't like AI" | "Fair. It only answers calls that would otherwise get nobody. A human is always the first choice if you're free." |

## 8. What they must answer for the agent to be built
The full list is the website setup form (`setup.html`, 15-20 minutes) and `docs/customer-setup-questionnaire.md`. For a barbershop, the essentials are:
1. **Basics:** shop name, address, owner name, email for summaries, billing email, mobile number for alerts.
2. **Hours:** open days and hours; holidays; time zone.
3. **Barbers:** names, which days each works, whether customers book a specific barber.
4. **Services:** every service with exact name, price and length (we start from their Square page; they confirm). Which combos to offer.
5. **Booking rules:** how far ahead, walk-ins accepted, gap between appointments, how many chairs at once.
6. **Policies:** late-cancel or no-show fee, deposits, age rules (kids' cuts, senior cuts), payment methods.
7. **Calendar:** where bookings live today (Square, Google Calendar, other) and who has the login.
8. **Phone:** who they pay for the business line, who has the login (for call forwarding), whether they want the AI only for missed calls or for every call.
9. **Voice and wording:** greeting, anything it must never say, languages (Spanish?).
10. **Consent:** OK to record and transcribe calls, and that callers are told it's automated.

## 9. Before you contact anyone (checklist)
- [ ] Demo is tested end to end (booking, info-only call, cancel).
- [ ] The phone-number fix is live (no "ten digits" loop).
- [ ] Hours and time zone confirmed with the shop (or says "demo hours").
- [ ] Pricing decided, with a refund or pilot policy you'll honor.
- [ ] Terms and privacy reviewed by a lawyer before taking real payments.
- [ ] Recording-consent rules checked for the state you're calling in.
- [ ] A real mailing address for email footers.
