# Pricing question for other AIs (no prices included)

Copy everything below the line into another AI.

---

I'm starting a small business selling a done-for-you AI phone receptionist to local businesses in the US. I need help setting fair, profitable prices. Please suggest a monthly price for each plan, a price for each add-on, a setup fee structure, and an overage rate. Explain your reasoning, compare with competitors, and tell me what you would change.

## What the product is

An AI voice agent that answers phone calls for a small local business, talks naturally, books appointments into the business's calendar, takes messages, and emails the owner a summary of every call. It can also answer questions about services and prices, cancel or reschedule appointments, handle emergencies for trade businesses (alert the on-call person), and keep a call log with a report (business hours vs after hours, booked vs messages).

The business keeps its existing phone number. Calls reach the AI through call forwarding set on the business's phone line, or the AI answers every call. I set up everything for the customer (done-for-you). The customer's only job is changing a few phone forwarding settings, and I walk them through it.

Target customers:
- Barbershops and salons (average ticket about $27 to $39 per haircut; booking is by appointment; walk-ins also welcome)
- Repair shops and trade businesses such as plumbers, electricians, HVAC (a single job is often worth hundreds of dollars; emergencies matter)
- Later: landscaping and similar local services

## What each customer gets in every plan

- A phone number for the AI (a small monthly cost that I cover)
- Booking into their calendar (conflict checks, hours, service lengths, several people working at once)
- A summary email after calls that matter, and a call log with a report
- Pricing answers from their own price list (the AI never invents prices)
- Cancel, reschedule, and check an appointment
- A heads-up email to me (and optionally them) when they reach 80% and 100% of their included minutes
- Done-for-you setup, testing, and a walkthrough of the phone forwarding
- About 30 days of small wording tweaks after launch

## Plans (differ only by included minutes per month)

- Starter: 300 minutes (about 120 calls)
- Standard: 500 minutes (about 200 calls)
- Plus: 750 minutes (about 300 calls)
- High volume: 1,200 minutes (about 480 calls)
- Minutes beyond the included amount are billed as overage (per minute)
- A typical call lasts about 2 to 3 minutes
- Month to month, no long contract

## Add-ons

1. Spanish and English: the AI starts in English and switches to Spanish when the caller needs it. I test it with a native speaker using the shop's own services before promising it.
2. AI answers every call (main line) with transfer to a human: instead of only catching missed and after-hours calls, the AI picks up every call, day and night. During open hours, if the caller asks for a person, the AI tries to connect the call to a number the owner chooses (it must not be a number that forwards back to the AI). If nobody answers, the AI stays on the line and finishes (books, takes a message, or schedules a callback). Outside open hours, it says the business is closed and offers to take a message or schedule a callback. Shops that use this mode use many more minutes.
3. Extra phone number (for a second location or a separate line)

Possible later add-ons (not built yet): text message confirmations to callers, text or phone-call alerts for emergencies, payment links by text, a weekly report emailed to the owner, direct connection to the shop's booking software (for example Square).

## Setup (one-time) options

- Standard setup: collect their answers to a questionnaire, build their settings and AI agent, test it, and walk them through the phone forwarding. English only, AI catching missed and after-hours calls.
- Extra setup work for Spanish (native speaker testing, extra prompt tuning)
- Extra setup work for AI answering every call with transfer (extra phone setup and a real phone test of the transfer)
- Custom or complicated setups (business phone systems, many services or staff): quoted separately
- Idea for the first customer or two: discount or waive setup in exchange for a testimonial

## Plan fit by business type

- Barbershop catching missed calls: probably Starter
- Barbershop with AI answering every call: Plus or High volume
- Repair shop or trade business catching missed calls: probably Standard
- Repair shop or trade business with AI answering every call: High volume

## My costs (facts, not prices)

- Voice platform cost measured on my own bill: about $0.17 per minute of call time (range I've seen quoted: $0.13 to $0.31)
- Phone number: about $2 per month per number
- Workflow automation platform: a flat monthly plan, with a limit on workflow runs per month (about 7 runs per call); I haven't confirmed how this scales
- Google Workspace tools: free for my use so far
- Payment processing fees on what I charge customers: not yet counted
- My time: setup takes about 3 to 5 hours for the first customer and 1.5 to 3 hours after that; ongoing support is small
- Things I haven't verified: whether the voice platform keeps billing minutes while a call is transferred, and whether a real phone call costs the same as a web test call

## Questions I want your help with

1. What monthly price would you put on each of the four plans?
2. What would you charge for each add-on (Spanish, main line with transfer, extra number)?
3. What setup fee would you charge for the standard setup, and for each setup add-on? Should setup be the same for every customer or depend on the work involved?
4. What should the overage rate per minute be, and should it be the same for every plan?
5. Should I charge differently for barbershops than for repair shops and trade businesses, or the same by minutes?
6. How do these compare with competing AI receptionist services (Smith.ai, Ruby, Goodcall, Rosie, Dialzara, and others)?
7. Is a per-minute plan a good model, or should I price per call, per booking, or a flat unlimited plan?
8. What would you change about the plan structure, the add-ons, or how I describe it to customers?
9. What first-customer offer (pilot, discount, money-back, free month) makes sense?
10. What is the biggest pricing mistake a new seller like me could make here?
